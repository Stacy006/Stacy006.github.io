## Plot 1: Bigfoot Sightings by Year

This line chart shows the number of reported Bigfoot sightings by year. The x-axis represents the year, and the y-axis represents the number of reports. The chart shows relatively few reports before 1970, followed by a noticeable increase and a peak in the early 2000s.

I used quantitative encoding for both axes and added points to the line to make individual years easier to identify. A single blue color was used because the chart represents one variable over time, so a colormap was not necessary. In Python, I converted the date column into datetime format, extracted the year, removed records with missing years, and grouped the data to count reports for each year.

## Plot 2: Top 10 States by Bigfoot Sightings

This horizontal bar chart compares the number of reported Bigfoot sightings across the ten states with the highest report counts. Washington has the most reports, followed by California and Florida.

I used nominal encoding for the state names and quantitative encoding for the report counts. The bars are sorted in descending order to make comparisons easier. The chart uses orange to highlight a selected state and light gray for the others. These colors distinguish the selected category rather than representing a continuous numerical scale. In Python, I grouped the data by state, counted the reports, sorted the counts in descending order, and selected the top ten states.

<div id="vis2"></div>

## Interactivity

The first plot includes tooltips that display the year and number of reports when hovering over a data point. The second plot allows users to click a bar to highlight a particular state in orange while the other states become gray. This interaction helps users focus on one state and compare its report count with the others. Clicking outside the bars clears the selection.
